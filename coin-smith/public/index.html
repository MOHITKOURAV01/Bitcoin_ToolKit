7 · The PSBT

A Partially Signed Bitcoin Transaction is the unsigned transaction packaged with everything a signer needs to check it. Nothing here is signed, so it is safe to hand to a hardware wallet or a co‑signer.

Bitcoin in plain English

UTXO ("a coin")

Bitcoin isn't a balance — it's a pile of separate chunks you received. Each chunk must be spent whole, like handing over a whole banknote.

Coin selection

Choosing which chunks to hand over. Fewer, larger coins keep the transaction small and cheap.

Change

Spend a 100,000 sat coin to pay 70,000 and the rest comes back to you as a new coin — exactly like change from a banknote.

Dust

An output so small (under 546 sats) that spending it later would cost more in fees than it's worth. Creating one wastes money, so we fold it into the fee instead.

Fee rate & vbytes

Miners sell block space. You bid in satoshis per virtual byte, so fee = size × rate. More inputs and outputs mean a bigger, pricier transaction.

Send‑all

When the leftover is too small to be worth keeping, there's no change output and the whole remainder becomes the miner's fee.

PSBT

A standard envelope (BIP‑174) holding an unsigned transaction plus the details a wallet needs to verify and sign it safely.

RBF

Replace‑by‑Fee. Flagging each input with nSequence 0xfffffffd tells the network "I may resend this with a higher fee" — useful if it's confirming too slowly.

nLockTime

An earliest-spend time. Below 500,000,000 it means a block height; at or above, a unix timestamp. Wallets set it to the current height so a miner can't profitably re-mine your transaction.
